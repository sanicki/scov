Update: SCOV Transition-

ing to Northstar Software
The transition to Northstar (NS) software is
progressing steadily. Director Vittitoe’s article
is the visionary perspective, and this article
covers processes. Our staff is currently learning to navigate
the administrative modules, which include processing sub-
mittals for house modifications. These submittals are also
reviewed in NS by the Architectural Review Committee
(ARC). Covenant compliance (violations) will soon be man-
aged through NS as well. All relevant sections of our cov-
enants, overseen by staff and the Neighborhood Pride team,
have been entered into the system along with corresponding
documentation from our governing documents. This will help
identify violations and automate the process of sending re-

lated emails and letters.

Rental properties are being identified and added to NS,
and renters are being entered into the system. Home paint
colors and other module types have also been recorded. Ad-
ditionally, staff are learning how to handle property transfers
between sellers and buyers using the software.

One unexpected but beneficial addition to this transition is
the integration of the Facilities Maintenance work order sys-
tem into NS. The software previously used by that depart-
ment was due for renewal, so we opted to move to NS, which
already includes this module, resulting in cost savings. The
facilities maintenance team and staff responsible for placing
maintenance orders are currently being trained in the new
system.

Email communications, such as This Week in SCOY, will
soon be sent through NS as well. Staff are learning to use
the campaign module to design email templates. NS also
offers a new push notification feature that can be used for
alerts like unplanned pool closures and other time-sensitive
announcements.

The interim website migration was a success, thanks to
the expertise of Beccy Kirk and the SCOV IT Work Group.
While a few minor issues are still being addressed, the in-
terim site is functioning well and gives us time to prepare for
the next phase of NS.

The next phase will focus on resident engagement through
the resident portal and the launch of the new website. Our
resident SCOV IT team will host introduction and training
sessions ahead of the website's go-live date. Resident test-
ing and feedback will be gathered during this period, and
further details will be shared via an email blast once the time
line is finalized.

In the Member's Portal, you'll be able to view property de-
tails, update personal information, check account balances,
view violations, access the member contact directory (with
customizable visibility settings), purchase event tickets,
book rooms, reserve lap lanes, fitness classes, and make
tennis and bocce court reservations. There is also an app for
these features.

One of the good outcomes of this transition is that each
process has to be reviewed and, at times, adapted to the
new software's capabilities. It's challenging and exciting.
Thank you for your continued support as we navigate this
transition.

As many of you have noted, there is a tremendous amount
of remodeling in our community. Whether inside or outside,
renovation often brings temporary distress to neighbors who
experience such things as the daily traffic of delivery trucks,
parking on the sidewalks by contractors, improperly stored
building materials, and discarded debris that blows into ad-
jacent yards. The nuisance factor is amplified by lengthy
time lines that stretch into months. We want to heighten the
awareness of what is expected of residents and their con-
tractors.

The following is a reminder that should be discussed with
your contractor before beginning construction:

1. Residents are responsible for the actions of their con-
tractors.

2. Aclean work site is important.

Development Standards Section 2.1.e: During construc-
tion, debris shall be disposed of daily or stored in a covered
container located in the driveway. The container shall not
extend into the sidewalk area.

Although it is not part of the governing documents, it would
be considerate to have any building materials stored out of
sight in the garage or in the backyard of the house.

3. Contractors are encouraged to be respectful of the
neighbors. Aprons and adjacent sidewalks are not designed
to support heavy vehicles and parking should be on the
blacktop. Sidewalk repairs are the responsibility of the Town
of Oro Valley. Neighboring driveways should not be blocked.
Parking of trucks to prevent safety concerns should be ad-
dressed using safety cones, etc.

4. The Town of Oro Valley Town Code 10-1-4, 6. Noise:

Noise generated by construction, demolition, excavation
or grading according to the following restrictions: a. Con-
crete. The pouring of concrete and all associated work may
be conducted from April 15 through October 15 between the
hours of 5 am and 7 pm, and from October 16 through April
14 between the hours of 6 am and 6 pm, or as authorized
pursuant to an Extended Construction Hours Work Permit
(ECHWP) issued under Oro Valley Town Code Section 6-6-
2. b. All Other Construction. Within five hundred (500) feet of
a residential zone, all other construction may occur between
the hours of 6 am. and 6 pm. Construction sites more than
five hundred (500) feet from a residential zone may conduct
work between the hours of 5 am and 6 pm Construction
hours may be extended according to an ECHWP issued un-
der Oro Valley Town Code Section 6-6-2. The distance from
a residential zone shall be determined by measuring from all
property lines of the construction site.

5. Our Master Declaration 4.5 Nuisance provides the au-
thority for the enforcement of these policies.

Before you hire a contractor, please refer to this site for infor-
mation on hiring Arizona licensed and non-licensed contrac-
tors: www.azroc.gov.

You may also refer to the Consumer Referral Vendor List
on our SCOV website under Members/Member Services.

Metabolism

Exercise keeps your metabolism high. Metabolism nat-
urally slows as you age, so it's harder to avoid pounds
creeping up as the years pass. Luckily, scheduling regular
workout sessions helps you increase the total number of
calories you burn, helping you maintain a steady, healthy
weight. You'll torch more calories if you add in resistance
training to your routine at least a few times a week, since
working out with free weights or doing body weight ex-
ercises helps build muscle mass. The more muscle you
have, the higher your calorie burn and it stays higher all
day long, even after you've stopped exercising. We have
gym orientation every Thursday at 1 pm, so come down
and learn how to use the weight machines.

Don't get scammed. We've had several recent instances
in Sun City (SCOV) of expensive projects that proceeded
without approval and that did not meet our Design Stan-
dards or Town of Oro Valley code. Anyone can pretend to
be a contractor. They may pretend to have a state contrac-
tor license when they don't, so be sure to check https://roc.
az.gov/ to verify. They can pretend to know our standards
when they don't, so submit your plans on our SCOV website
for review by the Architectural Review Committee before the
start of any work. They may push you to sign right away or
demand large up-front payments, so take the time to check

rules associated with leaving the cart path with a golf cart.
Many golfers seem to prefer driving down the entire length
of a hole on the grass. For those golfers who insist on driv-
ing the length of the hole there are gates where carts must
pass through to leave and return to the cart path. Once the
cart leaves the path through the gate it must drive straight
to the fairway. The cart must remain in the fairway until it
reaches the other end of the fairway where it must leave
through the exit gate. The goal of the gate system is to pre-
vent carts from creating a worn path parallel to the fairway.
