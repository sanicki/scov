Copper Center Update - January 2026
This update focuses on soft costs — often
misunderstood by those of us who are not
in the construction field. Our Schematic De-
sign Plan estimated $2,153,633 in construc-
tion (hard) costs such as labor, materials, HVAC, plumb-
ing, electrical, paint and demolition. In addition, there is
roughly $700,000 in soft costs, which are non-construction
expenses. These include building permits, architectural
and engineering fees, builder's risk insurance, construction
sales tax, etc. While items like equipment and furnishings
fall under soft costs and can be reduced, most soft costs
are unavoidable.

For example: Permits for a $2.2M project are approxi-
mately $30,000, including plumbing, HVAC, electrical and
kitchen removal. Architectural and engineering drawings are
required before work begins.

Something for Everyone
By Rick Price

Sun City is a community of family and friends. One of the
great things about our community is there is something for
everyone. If you have an interest, you most likely will find
some like-minded people who share your interest.

Sun City Oro Valley is a top-rated active adult community
in the United States. Approximately 40% of the residents in
Sun City have enjoyed some aspects of the golf facilities. In
such a diverse community, having 4 out of 10 residents en-
Joy golf in some capacity reflects well on the active lifestyle
here and the quality of the golf facilities we offer.

One of our goals at The Views Golf Club is to make the
game accessible to as many people as possible. We know
that golf is as much a social experience as it is an athletic
experience. Towards that end, we've created a wide variety
of activities for golfers and non-golfers alike.

For our traditional players, there are both 9-hole and 18-
hole leagues for the women and the men. These leagues are
fun and competitive; social storytelling, meals and an occa-
sional drink or two are standard practice after play is done.

For all levels of golfers, we offer several fun and easy
ways to experience what golf is all about:

+ If you’re a non-golfer or just beginning and even golf-
ers that want to improve, we offer weekly clinics on the full
swing, irons, short game and putting.

+ Our Chip & Putt league events are great times for getting
out on the course, chipping and putting on the course and
enjoying the spectacular views and social get together with
a meal after the round on the restaurant terrace. This event
has attracted over 1,500 registrants since its inauguration.

+ An instructional program called “OP 36” where players
can experience success by learning the game starting from
25 yards off the green. The goal is to score 36 or lower in
nine holes before moving back to 100 yards, then 150 and
so on.

* Strictly social events include Yappy Hours for our four-
legged friends and Demo and Club fitting days. Enjoy our
Fall and Winter Golf Demo Days, putting contests and even
a helicopter golf ball drop. Walk or run in our annual 5k fund-
raiser. Along with weekly barbeque cookouts on Friday and
Saturday at the Grille @ The Turn between the driving range
and 10th tee.

+ 18 holes of miniature golf course offers fun for adults and
kids of all ages.

Our most important promise to our residents and guests:
Agolfing experience that provides professional and friendly
service, mountain views and excellent conditions.

The Views Golf Club is one of the best maintained
and beautiful facilities in Arizona, thanks to Mike Kropf,
Course Superintendent and his dedicated team. We have
been awarded “The Best of the Northwest” golf course
numerous times.

Our best reward is having the residents of Sun City Oro
Valley enjoy our facilities. The number of golf rounds, rev-
enue and annual memberships continue to increase. We
welcome you to join us for all the fun activities and golf.

Walking Posture

If you are hunching your shoulders and looking down
while walking, you are reinforcing very bad walking posture
habits. Good walking posture is with the head up and eyes
forward. Bad posture can lead to low back pain, neck and
shoulder pain and doesn't allow you to take full, complete
breaths. It also reinforces the bad sitting posture many of us
have from hours in front of the computer. Your time walking
should be spent building good posture habits, not contribut-
ing to the damage you already do to yourself the rest of the
day. Every few minutes throughout your workout, give your
shoulders a backwards roll to check that you aren't hunch-
ing them.

Steps to Avoid Disappointment

If you are contemplating adding an expansion to your
home, please be aware that the steps to gain approval from
the Architectural Review Committee (ARC) are a bit more
involved compared to the regular ARC submittal process.

Please take time to read SCOV Board Policy ARC-006.005
if you plan on building a room addition, a patio cover or a
glassed-in patio enclosure. Screened-in patio enclosures
are not subject to this process.

The biggest difference between the regular ARC process
and the home expansion, patio cover or glass patio enclo-
sures process is that it takes more time to get approval. Most
expansion projects require a two-step process and neighbor
input is allowed for two weeks once the completed final sub-
mittal is received. Town of Oro Valley permitting may take
additional time. Wait to submit to the Town of Oro Valley until
your concept has been approved with a Concept Application
to the ARC and the Final Submittal is a go.

Making arrangements for ARC approval for a room ad-
dition, patio cover or glassed-in patio enclosure as part of
your early project planning will help you to start construction
sooner rather than later.

Happy New Year. January is the time of year
when the normal activities in the golf course
maintenance department really slow down. The
cooler temperatures decrease the growth rate of
the grass, so the amount of turf maintenance is
reduced. Less fertilizer, water and mowing are
required for maintaining good turf health. This
allows our department to catch up on other areas of the golf
course that we don't get to spend a lot of time on during the
rest of the year.

Several years ago, we started root pruning around tee
complexes and rough edges with our trencher. The turf on
some of these turf areas struggle with transition in the early
summer due to heavy golf traffic, limited sun from overhang-
ing trees and tree roots that compete with the grass roots
for water and nutrients. Over the years these branches are
continually trimmed, but the roots aren't removed very often.
The trencher cuts the tree roots outside the turf edge which
prevents the roots under the tree from competing with the
grass roots. This winter we will again be going around tees
that are heavily impacted by nearby trees.

Other items that will be addressed during this time frame
include moving sprinkler heads to the cart path on hole #4
to improve irrigation efficiency, raising sprinkler heads in
fairways that have sunk due to yearly sand topdressing and
dealing with chronic wet areas near greens and surrounds.
This work will be performed around play so be careful
of workers.
