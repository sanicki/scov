What makes our HOA community strong is
simple: neighbors treating each other with re-
spect, staying open with communication and

working together in good faith.

Questions and concerns are normal in any community.
What matters most is how we handle them. When we share
accurate information and ask questions directly, we build
understanding and avoid unnecessary confusion. When we
rely on assumptions or secondhand information, small gaps
can quickly turn into larger misunderstandings.

Rumors often begin when someone hears partial informa-
tion or sees something without full context. It's natural to try
to make sense of what we don't fully understand. A casual
conversation can be repeated and gradually takes on the
appearance of fact, even when key details are missing or in-
correct. Taking a moment to verify information with an official
source can often stop that process before it spreads.

Trust is the foundation of a healthy community. When mis-
information spreads, it can lead to unnecessary worry, frustra-
tion, or doubt and may cause neighbors to question decisions
or intentions without the full picture.

Board members are fellow homeowners who volunteer their
time to support the community. They welcome questions and
feedback, but inaccurate or incomplete information can make
their work more difficult and, at times, discouraging.

Rumors can also magnify small issues, turning routine
matters into larger concerns. This can lead to strained con-
versations, reduced cooperation and less productive meet-
ings where facts get lost.

Concerns about finances, projects or community decisions
can grow quickly when information isn’t clear or complete.
Staying grounded in verified updates helps everyone make

better informed judgments and maintain confidence in the
community's direction.

The SCOV Board and management team share up-
dates through This Week In SCOV, the SCOV website,
email communications, Board Meetings, and Board Chats.
These remain the most reliable sources for accurate and
timely information.

If something you hear raises concern, checking an official
source first, or asking a clarifying question, can often pre-
vent misunderstandings from spreading further.

Before sharing, it helps to pause and ask:

+ Is this confirmed?

+ Is it from an official source?

* Do | have the full context?

* Could this unintentionally create concern?

Strong communities aren't built on everyone always
agreeing. They're built on neighbors staying respectful, ask-
ing questions and listening to one another.

When we choose facts over assumptions and communi-
cate with care, we help maintain a community that is wel-
coming, stable and easier for everyone to enjoy day to day.

While the SCOV “snowbirds” are out of town for the sea-
son, there are plenty of birds still here loving the warm
weather. Here are some species to keep an eye out for when
you are out and about in the community.

First of all, Arizona is full of iconic native birds. The Gam-
bel's Quail is only found in the southwest region of the Unit-
ed States of America and northwestern Mexico. They are
instantly recognized by the beautiful topknot on the top of
both male and females’ heads. These little plump birds are
more commonly found running around the desert floor.

The illustrious hummingbirds are the favorite of many resi-
dents of SCOV. The Anna's Hummingbird is a very common
hummingbird to spot in your neighborhood throughout sum-
mertime. They do not migrate often, so they are in Arizona all
year round. These tiny birds flutter their little wings so quick-
ly it seems like they're hovering above the brightly colored
flowers they drink nectar from.

Flying high above Rancho Vistoso Boulevard, you can see
the Red-tailed Hawk which are the most common species of
hawks and one of the largest birds in North America.

Waterbirds are common to see around the ponds in the
golf course. Cormorants and ducks are frequently spotted
there. If you are lucky, you may even spot a Great Blue Her-
on fishing for food with their long beaks and graceful legs.

To learn more about the species of birds found around
SCOV, visit our birder club's website: https://scovbirding.

Theda Ellis, 302-388-9798, tellisscov@gmail.com
Why is the Cop;

Center Important?

] Returning to SCOV in time for the Fourth of
July concert reminded me once again why |
love living here. It was an afternoon that cap-
tures the spirit of our community; neighbors
gathering to celebrate our country, enjoy great music and
spend time together. It was SCOV at its best.

One of the things | value most about our community is
that there is no single “right” way to enjoy retirement. Some
residents spend their days on the golf course or at the pool.
Others, like me, enjoy traveling in an RV. Many find purpose,
friendship and creativity in our clubs. Every one of those in-
terests contributes to making SCOV the vibrant community
itis.

As a community, we have made significant investments
in many of our amenities. We've upgraded and maintained
the AFC, our pools and the golf course, and we've devoted
considerable attention to the restaurant. Those investments
matter. At the same time, another part of our community has
quietly continued to outgrow its space.

Our artisan clubs, including the Wood Shop, Thimbelenas,
Stained & Fused Glass and the Wood Carvers, have wel-
comed increasing interest from new residents, yet some are
now forced to limit participation because they simply don’t
have enough room. This isn’t a new challenge. We recog-
nized the need in 2018 when the first Artisan Center Task
Force began studying ways to provide additional space.

That need was one of the reasons SCOV purchased Cop-
per Center. While progress has taken longer than any of us

anticipated, the purpose has remained the same: to create
space that allows more residents to participate and more
clubs to thrive.

I've heard some residents say that the Copper Center will
benefit only a handful of people. Rather than speculate, we
worked with the office to compile the membership lists for
the clubs that will directly benefit from the project. After re-
moving duplicate memberships, we found 1,139 individual
residents—almost 33 percent of our residents. That's one
out of every 3 residents who would benefit directly. That in-
cludes the additional space in the Artisans Center that will be
created for the Carvers and expansion opportunities for the
Wood Shop, Gift Shop, Clay Club, and Library.

To me, Copper Center has never been just about a build-
ing. It's about investing in the people who make this commu-
nity special. As our interests evolve and new residents arrive
with different talents and passions, | believe it's important
that SCOV continues to provide opportunities for all of us.
That's one of the ways we ensure our community remains
as vibrant tomorrow as it is today.

What is Functional Fitness?

Functional Fitness is one of the most recent training trends
to hit the gym scene and it has exploded in popularity due to
the results that it produces. Functional Fitness is designed
to prepare your body for everyday living. It focuses on com-
pound movements that force you to engage all of your core
muscles and your stabilizer muscles, many of which are
not stimulated during traditional weight training. Beginning
a functional training program, or even incorporating a few
days of functional training into your current workout sched-
ule will save you a lot of time, pain and medical bills in the
future and it will make your life easier to live.

We often get questions about how to tell if your side and
back walls are on your property, your neighbor's or HOA
property, or shared. In Sun City two simple indicators might
help. At the corner of your lot, there might be a piece of rebar
in the ground, assuming no one removed it over the years.
The other indicator you can look for is a small metal disk at-
tached to the top of the masonry walls. The owner of the wall
can be determined by the location of the disk on the wall.

While these are quick indicators of where your property
boundaries might be, the only sure way to accurately de-
termine your property boundaries is to have the property
surveyed. While there is some expense involved in having
a professional surveyor come out to do this, it can quickly
resolve issues of who is responsible for painting a fence,
maintaining a wall, or whether the wall is a party wall and
jointly owned by you and your neighbor or Sun City.

If any owner is proposing to modify, make additions, or
build a wall on their property, they must obtain prior written
approval from the ARC and any neighbors directly affected
by those changes. Having a survey done is a critical step
before seeking the required approvals or any work starts.
Please see Master Declaration 4.15 for more details regard-
ing party walls.
