## Page 3

Tap into your creative side with the silversmithing and lapidary
workshops, art studios, the genealogy lab or the computer
learning center.

If you love to read, the community library has more
than 15,000 titles. The Institute of Learning in Retirement
provides a broad range of educational experiences in an
encouraging format right here on site.

Location Tucked inside a master-planned community just
north of Tucson, Arizona, Sun City Oro Valley is close enough
to the advantages of a large city without being too close. Our
residents say the distance fits their lifestyle: close enough to
reach all the attractions, including the University of Arizona,
while enjoying the quiet beauty of a suburban setting. The Town
of Oro Valley was recently ranked the 8th safest in the U.S.

Three mountain ranges surrounding Sun City Oro Valley
serve a visual feast of unique desert plants and animals. The
tallest range, the Catalina Mountains, is awash in vivid colors
daily, courtesy of the beautiful hues of Sonoran Desert sunrises
and sunsets. Hikers, bicyclists, walkers and nature enthusiasts
enjoy a variety of trails, winding sidewalks and scenic golf cart
paths throughout the community.

## Page 4

Site Sun City Oro Valley, sits on a 1000-acre site with 36% of
the space allocated to common area. The Views golf course
and the nearby Catalina Mountains are focal points. Urgent
care, medical/diagnostic services and shopping centers are lo-
cated nearby. Fire and police stations with emergency vehicles
are located within the community.

Houses There are 2488 homes in the community ranging
from 1000 to 2700 square feet with resales priced from the
mid $100s to the mid $500s. All houses have sidewalks and are
‘one story. Some houses are available for rent and listed in the
Tipster’s classified ads and on our referral board and website.

People There are over 4000 residents, ages 45 and up. Over
60% of the residents live here year round. Most people will
cite the community’s location, well kept appearance and the
large number of activities as their reasons for moving here. We
are a quick commute to Tucson for our working population.

Buildings Our Activity Center includes a 450 seat
auditorium with an audio visual system, a dance studio,
TV lounge and meeting rooms. Satellite recreation facili-
ties house large and small meeting rooms. WiFi is available

in all of our buildings. Amenities include state of the art
fitness center, two swimming pools, eight tennis courts,
five pickleball courts, five bocce courts, an artisan center,
library, computer classroom, Café, pro shop and a 9-hole
miniature golf course.

## Page 5

Infrastructure Streets, water lines and sewer lines are

owned by the Town of Oro Valley. Because of this it is not the
community’s responsiblitiy to repair or maintain them.

Operations All homeowners are members of Sun
City Oro Valley Homeowners Association. Seven elected
residents serve as its Board of Directors to establish policy,
approves budgets and assures the financial integrity of the
Association. A general manager and a professional staff
are responsible for daily operations and maintenance.
More than 22 volunteer committees assist the Board in many
areas. Sun City Oro Valley is an active adult community operat-
ing under age restrictions for those 45 years and older.

Finances Total assets exceed $20 million with annual
revenues of more than $6 million. Annual fees per household are
very competitive! Our Asset Reserve is adequately funded for
repairs and replacement of capital items, and Sun City Oro Valley
has NO debt.

Community Foundation The foundation is a 501(c)3
qualified charitable foundation. It was created in 1998 and as-
pires to make a unique value-added contribution toward el-
evating and expanding the life experiences of SCOV residents.
To date the foundation has received over 1800 donations total-
ing more than $500,000.

TheViewsGolfClub.com

There's a good reason why it’s called The Views Golf Club at
Oro Valley. In the shadows of the Catalina Mountains, a fresh,
clean breeze drifts off the desert and invites you toward the
unique beauty of the award-winning | 8-hole golf course.

A spectacular experience in desert golf, The Views has earned
a reputation as a hidden gem in the rich Southern Arizona golf
scene. Starting with the elevated tee shot from the first tee to
the challenging approach on number 18, the course will test
your skills. With multiple sets of tees on every hole, you can
choose your own challenge.

A community-owned course, The Views offers annual passes,
punch cards and daily fee rates. The Views allows members to
use private electric carts on the course and offers rental carts.
From the full-service pro shop, golfers can arrange lessons from
PGA professionals or practice on a full swing driving range and
the complete short game practice area. The Views offers 18
and 9 hole leagues for men and women. Weekly events mix all
members while couples can play various tournaments, mixers
and special events
through the year.

Area Attractions

University of Arizona Advanced learning oppotunities, cul-
tural and sporting events.

Arts

Tucson Museum of Art & Historic Block features dis-
plays of its fine collections as well as traveling exhibitions.

Tubac once a Spanish presidio site; today an artists’ community.

Family Fun
Colossal Cave is considered to be the world’s largest dry cavern.

Mt. Lemmon offers winter skiing, a chance to see the leaves
turn color in the fall and a place to cool off in the summer.

Old Tucson Studios a movie replica of 1860s Tucson theme park
Reid Park Zoo exhibits over 300 species of animals.

Heritage and Culture

AZ Historical Society Museum follows the development
of AZ from the first Europeans in 1540, through the flags of
Spain and Mexico, to 1800s territorial AZ.

Mission San Xavier del Bac considered The White Dove of
the Desert, impressive example of Spanish mission architecture.

Tombstone “the town too tough to die”, a National Historic Site.

Nature and Ecology

AZ-Sonora Desert Museum famous “living” museum housing
over 1300 kinds of plants and 300 species of animals.

Sabino Canyon features exhibits of the geology, history, and
nature lore of the region, hiking trails and picnic areas.

Tohono Chul Park is a 49-acre desert preserve with art gal-
lery, restaurant and gift shops.

State Parks

Catalina State Park hiking and horseback trails, guided bird
walks, wildflower walks, camping and picnic areas.

Kartchner Caverns State Park is a “live” cave.
Saguaro National Parks hiking trails, picnic areas.

Science and Technology

Biosphere 2 Center environmental research facility with
tropical rain forest, savanna, desert, salt water ocean and
a human habitat.

Kitt Peak National Observatory the world’s largest
collection of ground-based optical telescopes, researching
astronomy.

Pima Air and Space Museum over 250 restored vintage
aircraft represent America’s aviation history. Tours available.

Titan Missile Museum the formerly active Titan ICBM
complex, the only U.S. site that has not been destroyed.
