Our mission as the SCOV Board is to lead in maintaining
and enhancing the quality of life in Sun City Oro Valley as an
active adult community.

All Board minutes, agendas and reports are available online.
Board meetings are open to residents.

The Sun City Oro Valley Community Foun-
= dation was organized in 1998 and became an
IRS 501(c)(3) non-profit tax-exempt charitable
organization in 2000, which allows donations
to be tax deductible. The Mission of the Foun-
dation is to improve the quality of life for SCOV residents
and surrounding communities by providing funds for various
items and projects. The funds come from donations, gener-
ously, provided by our residents. This past year over 300
residents provided funding for $50,961 that was awarded in
support of SCOV clubs, SCOV organizations and outside
organizations with connection to a SCOV club. The Founda-
tion does not give grants to individuals.
Last year the Foundation gave grants to the SCOV Library,
SCOV Golf Course, Vistoso Photo Club, Bocce Club, Holi-
day Lights, SCOV Garden Club, SCOV Stained & Fused
Glass Club, Men's and Women's Golf Clubs, Vistoso Cy-
clists Club, Wheels for Kids, SCOV Charity Works for the
Emerge Center Against Domestic Abuse and the Pickleball
Club. The Foundation does a lot more for our community
with the Shred-it and Dispose-a-Med events. This year they
added an Electronic and Hazardous Materials disposal
event. Grants are not given for items that should be from the
SCOV operating budget or from any of its funds.
The Foundation is composed of 8 to 9 SCOV residents that
volunteer, serving staggered three year terms. The Founda-
tion members are also donors to the Foundation. If you think
your organization has a need for help from the Foundation
you can apply on their website at www.FoundationSCOV.
org or by contacting Charles Rommell, President, at Foun-
dationSCOV@gmail.com.

The Foundation will accept any donation no matter the
size. Why not help?

vital tool that will guide the organization's operations and ini-

viewing the draft budget proposal, discussing potential ad-
justments, and identifying key priorities. This will allow the

sure that the budget reflects the organization's strategic ob-
jectives.

The second meeting on April 22nd will allow the Board to
finalize discussions, approve the budget, and set a formal
path forward. By holding two separate meetings, the Board
aims to give ample time to address any concerns, foster col-
laboration, and ensure a comprehensive and well-consid-
ered budget is adopted

Build your Endurance

Have you ever been winded after a bike ride, found your-
self panting during a run, or felt tired after climbing the stairs?

The way your body responds to physical exertion is linked
to your level of endurance. Endurance, along with balance,
strength, and flexibility, make up the four core elements of
personal fitness. Endurance exercises are also commonly
referred to as aerobic exercises because they require the
pumping of oxygenated blood to the working muscles. Aero-
bic exercises cause you to breathe faster and more deeply
while also raising your heart rate.

Endurance training can be intimidating at first, especially
if you haven't attempted a lot of medium-intensity workouts
in the past, but you'll be glad to know that aerobic exercises
encompass a broad range of activities including speed walk-
ing, jogging, running, biking, swimming, dancing and kick-
boxing.

Building up your endurance can be fun if you take it slow
and safe. First, make sure the body is warmed up by taking
a short walk or jogging before engaging in any form of aero-
bic exercise. Next, start with 10-15 minutes of an aerobic
exercise of your choosing such as jogging, swimming, or
spinning on a stationary bike. Afterward, take stock of how
you feel and listen to your body; you don’t want to overexert
yourself. Remember to take it one day at a time and con-
tinue building upon your progress by increasing the time or
intensity of your aerobic workouts.

these areas of disturbance. During the construction phase,
those irrigation lines will be capped off to prevent damage.
