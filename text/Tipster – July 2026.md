From the Board of Directors

Our mission as the SCOV Board is to lead in maintaining
and enhancing the quality of life in Sun City Oro Valley as an
active adult community.

All Board minutes, agendas and reports are available online.
Board meetings are open to residents.

Earl Vittitoe 309-360-1601 earlw@gmail.com

With recent court decisions and pending leg-
islation, it is time to review how we conduct
meetings at SCOV. The Board and Admin-
istration are firmly committed to obeying all
open-meeting laws while robustly protecting
the private information of our community members. We up-
date our governing documents as soon as possible when
legislation is clarified, but we execute necessary operational
updates immediately.

All chartered committees and clubs must follow both the
law and our SCOV governing documents, some more close-
ly than others Clubs and groups that do not make Board rec-
ommendations, do not fall under the open meetings law, but
must follow Arizona privacy law as well as the Code of Con-
duct. Under Arizona law and recent binding court decisions,
association Boards must make decisions transparently.

This transparency was heavily reinforced by a landmark
Arizona Court of Appeals ruling in April 2026 (AZNH v. Sun-
land Springs), which clarified two massive points for all
HOAs:

* No Secret Voting: While boards can still enter a closed
executive session to discuss sensitive topics (like legal
advice or personal member data), absolutely no voting
or formal action can happen behind closed doors. These
sessions can legally be used to develop and refine mo-
tions—critical when member privacy is a factor—but every
final vote must take place in an open session where mem-
bers can observe and speak first.

+ Specific Agendas: Agendas for closed sessions must

provide members with enough clear, reasonable detail to

know what is being discussed without violating confidenti-
ality. Cryptic statutory citations are no longer enough.

The Board and Management immediately consulted legal
counsel to ensure we perfectly meet these rigorous require-
ments while protecting resident privacy.

To be subject to these open meeting requirements, a gath-
ering must involve a quorum of the Board, or be a standing
committee charged with developing formal policy recom-
mendations. This explicitly excludes operational working
groups assigned to perform administrative tasks (such as
gathering vendor bids, reviewing technical specs, or coordi-

nating daily club activities). Because these groups lack the
authority to make final decisions, they do not constitute a
statutory meeting.

At SCOV, our commitment is to keep our processes per-
fectly aligned with these high standards of transparency, en-
suring every member's voice is protected and heard.

Sandy Seddon sseddon@suncityorovalley.com 520-917-8080

As summer arrives, work at SCOV is certain-
ly not slowing down. We continue to have many

ing community infrastructure and addressing future drainage
concerns proactively.

Celebrating America’s 250th Birthday at SCOV
By Mariana Corella

On July 4, 1776, our founding fathers declared indepen-
dence from Great Britain with the adoption of the Declara-
tion of Independence. The 13 American colonies would no
longer have political ties with Great Britain, and the colonies
became a self-governing entity. This was only the beginning
of the long journey for the 13 colonies to become the Unit-
ed States of America we know today. It has now been 250
years since that fateful day and here at SCOV we are excit-
ed to celebrate such a historical moment.

There are a myriad of events happening all around SCOV
for this Fourth of July. Residents will be decorating their
golf carts and unique vehicles red, white, and blue for the
Annual Fourth of July Golf Cart Parade. Residents will also
decorate their doors for the holiday for the Fourth of July
Door Decorating Contest (sponsored by the Democratic and
Republican clubs) in which prizes will be given to the first,
second, and third place winners. Finally, the Sun City Sing-
ers will be performing their “Celebration of Freedom” concert
this Independence Day. All these free events will take place
on Saturday, July 4 so don't miss out on the fun. For more
information on all these events, go to the “Coming Events”
section of the Tipster.

SCOV is proud and excited to celebrate this momentous
occasion in this country's history, and we cannot wait to cel-
ebrate with you.

The SCOV mobile app continues to grow with
new features designed to make finding resi-

update expands directory search capabilities

street name, state, or phone number. Entering a house num-

pation search field. Not many of you have entered your oc-
cupation (or former occupation), but if you enter that informa-
tion, you might find some like-minded folks to share a cup of
coffee with. This information can be updated in your profile.
If you have not already done so, download the SCOV app
from the Apple App Store or Google Play Store and explore
these enhanced directory features.

Project Approval Permits

Effective July 1, 2026 the Architectural Review Committee
(ARC) is implementing a new process to alert residents to
projects that have Association approval. With all the projects
going on in our community, we receive multiple phone calls
and emails asking if there was ARC approval for specific
projects.

In order to communicate the approval to all, the ARC is
issuing permits which must be hung in the street-facing win-
dow of a house that has an in-house or ARC written approval
associated with the work being done. Once a resident has
received approval for their submission, they will pick up the
permit at the Welcome Center and post it in the window when
the work begins until it is completed. Owners whose projects
were approved prior to July 1 should request a permit by
contacting Katy Mosier, kmosier@suncityorovalley.com.

Cleaning Gym Equipment
Before and After Your Workout

In the modern age, people have become a little more con-
scious when it comes to cleaning gym equipment and disin-
fecting it after use. Whether you're going to a fitness class in
person or using the equipment, the equipment needs to be
cleaned after each workout.

If you are in a gym with other people, it is common courte-
sy to clean the equipment for a variety of reasons. Nobody
wants to share sweat. Even if you are the only one who is
going to use the equipment, you should definitely wipe it
down. Using the wipes that are provided, try to get all the
awkward areas such as nooks and crannies as well as the
seams around the seat. Also focus on any area of high con-
tact. Please do not use the towel you used to wipe your face
with to wipe the machine down.

serve the desert environment and the architectural charac-

objects showing human workmanship) should not be placed
in front yards or street-facing side yards. Instead, artifacts

help keep our community looking great.

The 2026-2027 budget process began in the Finance and
Budget Committee in March, involving reviewing monthly
and year-to-date figures for fiscal year 2025-2026, and fore-
casts for this new fiscal year, resulting in three drafts. Sev-
eral board members regularly attend our two-hour monthly
meetings to get a better picture of the numerous financial
reports and the discussions within the committee. SCOV is
experiencing many of the same increases in costs that we
face; insurance, wages, fuel, food and maintenance/con-
struction. Each line item for every department is reviewed in
detail, to see where costs can be cut, without reducing the
quality of services and activities we enjoy in our community.
Income and expenditures for the reserve funds (mainly op-
erating, capital, contingency and reserve) are reviewed and
projected. The small increase in our HOA dues meets the
needs of the new fiscal year and the future needs of the
reserve fund. For $7.85/day, we have a wealth of amenities,
and most importantly a great community.

Fireworks are deeply stressful

for many dogs. Their sensitive
hearing makes the sounds far
more intense than what humans
experience. Signs of distress in-
clude trembling, panting, hiding
and attempts to escape, which
is why July 4 sees a spike in lost
pets. Preparation is key: secure
your yard, update ID tags and exercise your dog earlier in
the day. During the event, keep dogs indoors with music or
white noise playing. For severe cases, talk to your vet about
medication in advance. With patience and planning, both of
you can get through it.

We are often asked, “What is the difference between ping-
pong and table tennis?” The short answer is, “very little.” The
difference is mainly in the scoring and more formal rules re-
garding the serve. Table tennis is usually played to 11 points
(win by two) with each player having two serves (not two
tries, like in tennis). That change is very easy to handle, but
some folks may struggle with the serve that requires the ball
to be hit from behind the table end line and being tossed into
the air at least six inches by the palm of the hand before con-
tact. If you are a beginner you are forgiven, but you should
strive to follow the serve rules as you progress. Paddles
have changed over the years too, but there are nice foam-
backed paddles available for your use in the room. Come
