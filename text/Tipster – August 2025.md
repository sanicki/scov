To serve on an HOA Board of Directors
(BOD) , a homeowner needs to be a mem-
ber in good standing, not have a conflict of
interest and demonstrate a commitment to
the community. While there aren't universal,
state-mandated qualifications, common requirements in-
clude being a homeowner within the community, being up to
date on assessments and adhering to HOA rules.
Examples of qualifications:
-Homeownership: Board members are required to be
homeowners to ensure they have a vested interest in the
community's well-being.

-Member in Good Standing: This means being current on
assessments, not in violation of the governing documents.

-No Conflict of Interest: Members should avoid any situ-
ations that could compromise their ability to make impartial
decisions.

-Commitment to the Community: Board members should
be passionate about improving the association and dedicat-
ed to fulfilling the responsibilities of their role.

-Communication Skills: Effectively communicating with
other homeowners, handling disputes, and distributing as-
sociation news are crucial skills.

-Time Commitment: Serving on the board requires a sig-
nificant time commitment, including attending meetings, par-
ticipating in discussions and contributing to decision-making
processes.

-Ethical Conduct: Integrity, honesty and transparency are
essential qualities for board members.

Key Responsibilities:

-Policy Decisions: Board members are responsible for
making policy decisions that affect the community.

-Financial Oversight: The board oversees the associa-
tion's finances, including budgeting and accounting. The
board has a legal obligation called fiduciary duty to act in the
best interests of the association as a whole and its members.
This duty includes care and good faith, meaning they should
not take advantage of their position or make decisions that
benefit themselves at the expense of the community.

Lizards of Southern Arizona Diversity and Ecological Importance

Southern Arizona is home to a remarkable va-
riety of lizards, thanks to its unique blend of des-
ert, grassland, and mountain habitats. This diver-
sity makes the region one of the richest areas for
reptile life in the United States. These fascinat-
ing creatures are not only an important part of the
landscape but also play a vital role in maintaining
ecological balance.

Common Lizards of Southern Arizona

One of the most commonly seen species is the
Desert Spiny Lizard (On the Cover). With its bold,
spiny scales and vibrant colors, this lizard is of-
ten spotted basking on rocks or tree trunks. It is
a highly adaptable species, thriving in both desert
scrub and urban backyards.

The Common Side-blotched lizard is another
widespread resident. These small lizards, rec-
ognizable by the dark blotch behind each front
leg, are abundant in open desert areas. They
are quick-moving and well camouflaged, making
them a favorite snack for many predators.

_ The Zebra-tailed lizard stands out for its distinc-
tive black-and-white banded tail. Known for its
speed, it raises its tail high when running across
hot desert sand. This species prefers open areas
with sparse vegetation, where it can spot preda-
tors from a distance. ;

_ One of Southern Arizona’s most colorful reptiles
is the Collared Lizard. with its bright green or yel-
low body and black collar bands around the neck,
it is both striking and powerful. Collared lizards
are skilled hunters, preying on insects and even
smaller lizards.

The Greater Earless lizard is another fast-
moving species that lacks external ear openings.
These lizards are well adapted to desert life, of-
ten sprinting across open areas and blending into
sandy soil.

In rocky areas and canyons, the Chuckwalla
can be found. This large, herbivorous lizard feeds
primarily on desert plants and is famous for wedg-
Ing itself into crevices when threatened.

Ecological Importance of Lizards

Lizards are far more than just a familiar sight in
Southern Arizona—they are a key part of the eco-
system. One of their most important roles is as
insect controllers. Many species consume large
numbers of grasshoppers, ants, beetles and other
insects, helping to regulate pest populations that
could otherwise damage vegetation.

_They also serve as a critical food source for a va-
rey of predators. Birds of prey, snakes, coyotes,
and even larger lizards depend on them for Suste-
nance. This makes lizards an essential link in the
desert food web.

Some species, like the Chuckwalla, help with
seed epee: By feeding on flowers, fruits, and
leaves, they contribute to plant reproduction and
help maintain the desert’s native vegetation.

Lizards also act as bioindicators—their pres-
ence, abundance, or absence can indicate chang:
es in environmental health They are sensitive to
temperature, habitat disturbance and pollution,
monitoring lizard populations can give scientists
valuable information about the impacts of climate
change and human activity.

While most lizard species in this area are not
endangered, their habitats face pressures from
urban development, invasive plants, and climate
change. Maintaining healthy desert ecosystems is
crucial for their survival. Homeowners can support
lizards by preserving native vegetation, avoiding
pesticide overuse, and providing natural shelter
such as rocks and brush.

Southern Arizona's lizards are as diverse as the
landscapes tiey inhabit—from tiny side-blotched
lizards darting through gravel to robust chuckwal-
las sunning on rocky slopes. Each species plays
an important role in controlling insect populations,
sup} ortnig Predators and maintaining plant health.
Protecting their habitats not only safeguards these
fascinating poptlee but also preserves the ecologi-
cal integrity of the entire region. By understanding
and valuing the role of lizards in Southern Arizona,
we take an important step toward sustaining the
health and beauty of our desert ecosystems for
generations to come.

happenings—like a pool or building closure—so you're nev-
er caught off guard.

Be the First to Know

Information is often sent to the app before it goes out via
email, meaning you'll get updates sooner and stay ahead of
the curve.

Already on the Website? You're ready to go.

If you already have login credentials for the SCOV website,
you're all set—use the same credentials for the mobile app.
Download it today from your app store and discover how
easy and convenient staying connected can be.

Development Standards and ARC Submittals

Just a reminder that it is important to request ARC approv-
als for exterior projects. When you sell your home, you will
thank yourself for being proactive when you are trying your
to complete an escrow.

Please complete all of the process by providing all items
at once. It is best not to submit until you have decided on a
contractor and have a written estimate to included in your
submittal. We are not interested in the pricing. Feel free to
black out that information. What we need from the vendors
are usually drawings and complete descriptions including all
materials, colors and dimensions. Missing information will
delay your approval so please check with the Architectural
Coordinator if you have any questions.

Applying on the website, remember this is a two-step pro-
cess. The first step you are requesting a plot plan and the
correct form by identifying the project. No details are needed
then. Once the plot plan and form for your project are sent
back to you from project@suncityorovalley.com, you will
then provide all details of the project. Your complete form
and backup information are received by us, it will go on the
next ARC agenda. The ARC meets the second and fourth
Tuesday of each month; the deadline for submittals is the
Tuesday prior to the meeting.

Counteracting the Effects of Aging

Many of the changes in our musculoskeletal system re-
sult more from disuse than from simple aging. Stretching
is an excellent way to help maintain joint flexibility. Weight
training can increase muscle mass and strength, enabling
people to continue their daily routine activities without maxi-
mal exertion. Just 30 minutes of moderate activity incorpo-
rated into your daily routine, can provide health benefits.

An exercise program doesn't have to be strenuous to be
effective. Walking, square dancing, swimming and bicycling
are all recommended activities for maintaining fitness as
we age.

The Master Declaration, Tract Declarations and Develop-
ment Standards 2.18.C.4 provide a pretty clear direction for
managing greenery within one’s yard. Besides weed control,
we have a duty to be neighborly and promote safety.

Example: Within the front yard setback, 3 feet 6 inches is
the maximum height of any bush/hedge. This keeps visibility
between drivers and walkers within a measure of safety.

For side and backyards, the maximum height is 6 feet. This
height keeps plants under control and mitigating scenery vis-
ibility impairments for one’s neighbors. For some, manag-
ing neighbor visibility is even more critical. For example, Unit
15 (Cirrus Hill Dr, Tumble Brook, Meteorite Trail, and half of
Trade Winds Way) has a maximum of 3 feet high for shrub-
bery that is within 15 feet of the back yard property line.

This summer was another growth year for our greenery.
Please take the time to enjoy that growth while also showing
neighborly consideration.

Why It's So Important to Regularly Trim
Palm Trees in Arizona's Desert Climate

Arizona's desert climate brings intense heat, strong
winds and occasional storms, which can make poorly
maintained palm trees a significant safety concern. Dead
fronds, which can weigh several pounds, may fall unex-
pectedly, posing risks to people, pets, vehicles, and near-
by structures. Additionally, overgrown palm trees are more
susceptible to losing fronds during storms or even becom-
ing uprooted, potentially causing extensive damage. An-

Did you know that in the past, young ladies (even from
affluent households) spent a significant time training to be
good seamstresses? They had to if they wanted to wear
clothes. A shirt took a day to finish. The first sewing ma-
chines were not made for the home. The exorbitant price of
those machines made it impossible to buy them. English-
man Thomas Saint laid the groundwork for the machine in
1790. But American Isaac Singer in 1851 made significant
improvements to machine technology, and innovations came
swiftly. By the 1900s, affordable home-use machines started
to sell. Women could sew clothes for themselves or others,
and make money. The sewing machine not only transformed
the garment industry, but altered social dynamics around the
world making clothing more accessible. Today a shirt takes
an hour to complete. Come see our modern sewing, serger,
long arm quilter and embroidery machines. And yes, we still
hand sew.
