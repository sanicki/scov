Copper Center Update

Theda Ellis, 302-388-9798, thedaellis@me.com

Are we, Sun City, willing to wait until 2032
') for the Copper Center renovation to be com-
plete?

When | ran for the Board, the biggest ques-
tion was always what will we do with the
Copper Center building? The answer hasn't changed. It will
become a center for our artisans and clubs. Realizing that
vision takes funding, we won't begin until the resources are
in place.

The decision to purchase has been made by our commu-
ity twice: Now the question is how quickly we can bring it
to life.

At our current pace, the Copper Center won't be ready
until 2031 or 2032. Meanwhile, Artisan Center clubs are al-
ready feeling the strain of very limited space with growing
memberships. Remember, our artisans have been asking
for additional space for almost a decade, since 2018. If you
missed the Artisan Center Open House, then you missed a
chance to see just how in need they are. We can and must
do better for them.

Fundraising gives us that opportunity. At this time in our
lives, we don’t know our future except we do know that most
of our lives are behind us. How do we want to spend our
time? How do we invest in the quality of our lives? Many of
us are financially comfortable. We can support our neigh-
bors and ourselves by investing in our own community with
donations to move the Copper Center forward. Every contri-
bution, no matter the size, moves the project forward. Dona-
tions are simple to make at the Welcome Center and we've
already begun with over $7,000 raised.

With plans and cost estimates coming soon, we'll have a
clear path ahead. The Copper Center isn’t just a building,
it's a vision and an investment in our community, creativity
and future.

Let's accelerate the timeline. Let's come together. Let's
make it happen.

Counting Calories

No matter what your goal is: losing weight, maintaining
your current weight or just trying to eat healthier, your best
bet is keeping track of the calories you intake. It's easy to
say “eat less, move more,” but without tracking your intake
of calories it can be more of a hit and miss than effective
dieting. While the endeav or of tracking your calories may
seem like a hassle, the benefits truly do outweigh the in-
convenience. It may seem like keeping track of the calories
you intake is a nuisance, confusing and even difficult but
with the right tools it can be made easier. To properly keep

track of your calories it's important to consult the nutritional
label of whatever you're eating and to accurately measure
your intake.

If you are leaving us for the summer, please take a mo-
ment to address any water features or pools you have on
your property before you go. Any standing water is a breed-
ing ground for mosquitos and will attract other pests and crit-
ters during the hot summer months. Fountains and ponds
are best drained and cleaned before you leave. You might
consider using a pool mosquito repellent tablet such as Mos-
quito Dunks or other products to prevent the growth of mos-
quito larvae in your pool. Secure covers that seal over the
pool are also helpful. Please remember that our Master Dec-
laration section 4.6 states: No owner shall permit anything or
condition to exist upon any lot which shall induce, breed or
harb or infectious plant diseases or noxious insects.

We thank you for helping us enjoy our summer here
at SCOV.

May is the time of the year when the golf
course maintenance department really starts to
get busy. The transition from winter ryegrass to
the summer bermudagrass is in full effect. The
ryegrass has been chemically treated and is be-
ing slowly removed from the fairways, tees and
green aprons as the bermudagrass is starting to

come out of dormancy. This year the extremely hot tempera-

tures that started in February and continued through March
has sped up the process.

Starting May 26, the fairways are going to be aerified with
large solid tines followed with a heavy topdressing. The s
and aeration and sand provide many benefits to the turf.
Compaction is relieved from all the golf cart traffic, oxygen
can get to the roots organic material is reduced and the
bermudagrass prefers growing in sand than the native de-
composed granite soils. The sand also provides a smoother,
firmer playing surface for the golfers. The topdressing piles
of sand will again appear in the main parking lot in early May.

May 18 and 19, the greens are going to be aerified and
topdressed similar to the fairways. However, the greens will
be aerified with smaller tines so the playing surface can heal

quicker. Something different this summer is the greens will
only be aerified twice to avoid disrupting the playing surface
as much. However, the organic matter that is maintained
with aerification still needs to be addressed. Instead of aeri-
fication, the greens will be verti-cut more frequently and fol-
lowed by light topdressing.

Keeping things moving makes the whole round feel better.
