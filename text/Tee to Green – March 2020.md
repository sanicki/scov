Message from the Director of Golf
Rick Price, PGA

We are excited to announce that The Views Golf Club will do a major golf course
reconstruction project starting the spring of 2020.

"IMPROVING OUR COURSE TO MAKE YOUR EXPERIENCE EVEN BETTER."

The schedule for reconstruction of the golf course greens, driving range tee, practice
putting green, and short-game area will start on April 27, 2020. The golf course will be
open during construction and played as a par 68 with all play starting on hole #10. The
course will play maximum distance of approximately 5,500 yards. There will be three
different sets of tees. The temporary greens are being prepared now to provide the best
quality putting surface as possible.

Our existing TifDwarf greens will be replaced with Tifeagle greens. The green complexes
will be cored out 18” below the surface. The green surrounds will be sodded, and the
green surfaces sprigged.

All new grass areas will NOT be over-seeded for the winter. A minimum of a year is
required to establish the new sod before winter rye-grass could be seeded into the
Bermuda grass, or it will be severely damaged the following season. There is a strong
possibility we will not over-seed the fairways and paint the fairways. Review USGA
video on painted fairways CLICK HERE. However, the tees will be over-seeded. It would
allow us to play the greens Labor Day Weekend in September and not close the golf
course for three weeks for over-seeding or have golf cart restrictions in the fall. We will
have more information about this operational decision soon.

The greens project is essential to the long-term success of our community and golf

Warmer temperatures are on the way. This has been a wet and cold winter so far, but the

Lesson Tee

Do you come up short on your approach shots? You think you have chosen the correct
club for the distance, but you consistently are chipping on to the green for your next shot
instead of putting for birdie?

What yardage are you playing to? Most golfers now use GPS to the flag and that’s good,
but what about knowing what the yardage is to the front or to the back of the green.
Some greens have a 3-4 club difference front to back. Next time you play try taking a 6
iron instead of a 7, or an 8 instead of a 9 iron. Club down a club. Doing that may put you
on the back of some greens but chances are you are not hitting every approach shot
flush. Plus, the extra club will make you swing easier and within yourself and this will help
with more solid contact.

Also, try aiming for the center of the green. Don’t go for that pin tucked behind a bunker.

Aim for the center and club down one club and you will stop coming up short and start
hitting more greens and putting for more birdies. Go play and have fun!
